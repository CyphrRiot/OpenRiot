## Release Overview

The crypto holdings table got honest. BTC stops rounding itself
into a clean number. The buy and sell limits stop reaching for
year-old highs that will never be touched again, and instead
anchor to real support and resistance the coins actually traded
through in the last ninety days. Along the way we removed a
column that existed for no one, dropped the dollar signs nobody
needed, and made the decimals line up like they were always
supposed to.

**Total changes: crypto price precision, honest buy/sell limits,
cleaner table.**

---

## 🔢 Crypto: The Table Tells the Truth

Four small fixes in `source/crypto/crypto.go`, none of them
glamorous, all of them overdue.

**BTC stopped rounding itself.** The CoinGecko call asked for
whole dollars by omission, so a coin worth $79,623.85 showed up
as $79,614. The request now asks for `precision=full`, and the
pennies come back.

**The decimals line up.** Price and Entry columns widened to ten
characters so a six-figure BTC price with cents doesn't shove the
row out of alignment. Buy and Sell Limit sit right-aligned with
commas, so every decimal point lands on the same mile marker.

**The `$` signs vanished.** Buy and Sell Limit were the only two
columns wearing a dollar sign while nothing else did. They now
show bare numbers — `446.40`, not `$446.40` — because the column
header already told you it was money.

**The `Dest` column is gone.** It told you where to rotate the
coin, advice that had been surviving the last several rebalances.
It was noise. Removed. The table has nine columns now, all of
them useful.

---

## 📏 Buy/Sell Limits: No More Ghost Numbers

This is the one that actually changed behavior. For months, the
sell limit reached back a full year, aiming at the highest candle
the coin had ever printed. SOL said sell at $235. BTC said sell
at $118k. Those were the ghosts of old highs — levels the market
hadn't touched in months and wasn't heading back to.

The machine now reads **the last ninety days**. Buy limit rides
the ninety-day support; sell limit rides the ninety-day
resistance, with a modest buffer. When the coin has already blown
through recent resistance, the machine doesn't silently point
backwards — it projects a **measured move**: the height of the
ninety-day range, added above the breakout. That's a level built
from the coin's own recent behavior, not a fixed percentage with
no memory.

And the buy side got a clamp. A coin that ran from $375 to $1,226
should never tell you to buy the dip at its ninety-day low of
$375 — that's not a dip, that's a crash nobody should wait for.
So the buy limit never sits more than twenty percent below the
current price. ZEC's buy target is now a sane pullback, not a
round trip to the bottom of the range.

The Feds, who love a clean round number, would have left it at
the year-old high and called it precision. We call that reading
the wrong page.
