# OpenRiot v8.0.9: Triptych, With Gaps

> _"A triptych is three paintings that refuse to be one painting.
> The hinges are the argument. The gaps are where the wall gets
> a vote. Hang it badly and you have a rumour. Hang it right
> and the middle panel holds the whole room."_
> -- The OpenRiot Crew, v8.0.9

---

## Release Overview

The old bar was one long plank with everything nailed to it:
workspaces, a window title, the weather, the date, eighteen
status icons, and a power button, all sharing the same 95%.
It worked the way a studio apartment works. You could sleep
there. You could not invite anyone over.

v8.0.9 cuts the plank into three panels. Workspaces and the
active window take the left. Weather and date take the middle.
The status icons take the right, where they can mutter among
themselves without elbowing the clock. The wallpaper shows
through the gaps, which is the entire point of a hinge.

The same release fixes an updater that treated one changed pin
like a gallery fire, and corrects a Wi-Fi table that had been
naming the wrong driver for Intel AX cards since before anyone
thought to check.

**Total changes: one plank became a triptych, one updater
learned to read the label, and one plaque stopped lying to
visitors.**

## The Hinges

Three polybar windows now stand where one used to lean. Each
has its own width, offset, corner radius, and background:

| Panel | Width | Hangs at | Job |
|-------|-------|----------|-----|
| Left | 38% | 0.5% | Workspaces and window |
| Center | 21% | 40% | Weather and date |
| Right | 37% | 62.5% | Status icons |

Every panel gets a 12px radius. The gaps land around 19 pixels,
enough wallpaper to read as three frames without turning the
top of the screen into a fence. Left and right share
`#9906070B`, the dark base at 60% opacity. The center drops to
`#3306070B`, the same color at roughly 20%, so the clock looks
like it is painted on the glass and the glass forgot to be
opaque.

The left panel gets 38% because window titles are liars. They
will take whatever you give them and then ask for a second
line. The center gets 21% because weather and date are the
only modules on the bar that know when to stop talking.

A triptych is not a subscription to a gallery. It is three
rectangles you own, in colors that live in a file on your disk.

## The Wall

Three frames still need one reserved strip. Otherwise the app
behind them writes its first line underneath the bar and
nobody finds out until a password prompt is missing its top
half.

`config/i3/config` now reserves 28 pixels at the top, matching
the scaled bar height at 1080p. The autostart line launches
`polybar left`, `polybar center`, and `polybar right`. The
watchdog checks each by name and restarts only the one that
fell off the wall. Losing the clock does not take the
workspaces with it.

`--polybar-setup` renders three bar sections from the same
template and applies the usual resolution scaling.
`Super+Shift+Space` still restarts all of them through IPC,
because that key already has a job and we are not training a
second one.

The upgrade path had one more hole. The installer cleanup
killed polybar and trusted the following i3 restart to bring
it back. i3 restart does not rerun `exec` lines, so the screen
came back with three bar sections and no bars.

The three bar launch lines are now `exec_always`, so they run
on login and on every i3 restart. The watchdog is
`exec_always` too, guarded by a pidfile so repeated restarts
do not stack loops on top of each other. `make-local` and
`setup.sh --local` leave a working bar behind instead of a
clean desktop and a question.

## The Restorer

`--pkg-update` used to read every pin in `packages.yaml`,
strip the names down to bases, and hand the whole list to
`pkg_add -Iu`. On a machine that already matched its pins,
that meant a slow walk through 103 packages for nothing. On a
machine with a flavored package, it did something funnier.

`angband-4.2.5-sdl` was reduced to `angband-4.2.5`. The
updater asked the mirror for a package by its birthday. The
mirror said "Problem finding angband-4.2.5." The updater took
this personally and stopped the tour.

The restorer now checks the installed full name against each
pin and skips exact matches. Only missing or genuinely
different packages go to `pkg_add`. A new
`config.PackageSpec` keeps the flavor while dropping the
version: `angband-4.2.5-sdl` becomes `angband--sdl`, which is
a package the mirror can actually find.

The install fallback uses the same spec. A unit test pins the
conversion down for `0ad`, `python`, `gtk+3`, and the rest of
the naming circus, so the next person who touches package
names has something to trip over.

## The Plaque

The Wi-Fi table had been telling AX200 and AX201 owners they
were running `iwm(4)`. They are not. Those cards use `iwx(4)`.
The plaque now says so, and it lists AX210 and AX211 with a
note that the CNVi variants need a matching chipset.

Broadcom's BCM43602 and BCM4378 joined under `bwfm(4)` with
`fw_update bwfm` required. BCM4360 and QCA6174 moved to the
do-not-buy list, where they can think about what they did.

The Navigate This Guide TOC gained an explicit
`supported-systems` anchor, matching the pattern every other
entry already uses. The donation address was replaced, and the
QR code was regenerated so the image and the string point at
the same wallet again.
