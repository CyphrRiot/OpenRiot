# OpenRiot v8.0.5: It Comes in Colors Everywhere

> _"The house was gray, the road was gray, and the little
> notifications were gray too. Then somebody opened a door
> and the whole street walked in wearing colors. The dog
> barked at the rainbow. The wizard, it turned out, was a
> file on disk."_
> -- The OpenRiot Crew, v8.0.5

---

## Release Overview

OpenRiot 8.0.5 repaints the desktop and fixes the plumbing underneath.
The palette tones down, the bar picks up a rounded outline and one pixel
of air, and the volume, microphone and brightness keys now answer on the
screen instead of whispering through a notification daemon.

It is a coat of paint with a torque wrench in the pocket.

**Total changes: one palette, one bar, one OSD, and the small arguments
in between.**

---

## A Coat of Many Colors

The canonical palette in `config/colors.toml` mutes the bright accents
into something that sits still at night. Because every template reads
that file, the change lands everywhere at once: the bar, the workspace
pills, rofi, helix, dunst.

The workspaces stop hardcoding their own weather. Focused, dimmed, and
urgent states now come from the palette, so the bar and the workspaces
finally agree on what green means.

The night-light icon learned to testify. Amber when the room is warm and
ordinary, a colder blue-purple when the filter is actually on. No more
squinting to guess whether the color cast is a feature or a mistake.

A theme here is a file you own. Not a subscription you renew, not a
palette the store can revoke. It lives on your disk and it survives the
next reboot, which is more than most colors can promise.

---

## The Display Is the Message

`openriot --osd` draws the change where your eyes already are.
Volume, microphone and brightness render as a small themed strip by
way of `osd_cat`, then vanish. When X isn't there, or the helper isn't
installed, it falls back to the old toast without complaint.

The pop-up was always somebody else's service. This one is a rectangle
we draw ourselves, in our colors, on our terms.

---

## One Pixel of Breathing Room

The bar now meets the top of the screen with no dead strip above it, and
stops one pixel short of the windows below so two outlines never touch.
`gaps top 1` reserves that breath, and `smart_gaps off` keeps it even
when a workspace holds a single window.

A lone window finally keeps its border. `hide_edge_borders none` sees to
that, so a full workspace no longer pretends to be a full-screen app.
The focused window outline is dimmer, too, closer to the room than to a
traffic cone.

---

## It Fits Every Screen

The font scaling markers in `polybar.Setup` pointed at sizes that were
never in the template, which meant half the bar ignored the resolution.
They now match `size=18` and `size=12`, so the bar scales its type on the
screens that aren't 1080p. The height still steps by width: 22, 26, 28,
32.
