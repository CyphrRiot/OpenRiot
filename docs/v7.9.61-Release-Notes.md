## Release Overview

The lock screen rotation got a fresh coat. Eighteen of the
forty-two frames were rebuilt on a new palette — deep purple
shot through with the OpenRiot lime green — over a modern
sci-fi backdrop, with "OpenBSD", "OpenRiot", and the version
number woven quietly into an ornate border, and "Locked"
sitting crisp above "Enter your password." Behind the glass,
two small but real fixes: the crypto sell column stops
sandbagging the ninety-day high, and WireGuard stops stuffing
duplicate nameservers into resolv.conf.

**Total changes: repainted lock screens, honest sell limits, a
resolver that cleans up after itself.**

---

## 🔒 Lock Screens: New Glass, No Noise

The old lock screens were fine. That was the problem. "Fine"
is where a product goes to be ignored, and the glass had been
doing exactly that since the last sweep. So it got repainted.
The new palette runs deep purple shot through with lime green,
each frame built over a modern sci-fi backdrop and framed edge
to edge by an ornate border that quietly carries "OpenBSD",
"OpenRiot", and "7.9" — present, never shouting.

And the readout is the one thing a lock screen must get right:
"Locked", large and legible, sitting over "Enter your
password", with nothing else on screen. No empty password
boxes, no menus, no taskbar, no date, no logos, no repeated
elements. Just the glass and the question.

---

## 📏 Crypto: The Ceiling Stops Lying

The sell column had a bad habit. It knocked three percent off
the ninety-day high before it would even consider letting a
coin sell there — a haircut the machine applied because it
didn't trust the ceiling it had measured. So the ceiling lied
a little, every time. That stops. The sell limit now sits on
the true ninety-day high, and the only time the machine
reaches beyond it is when price has actually broken above
that high — a measured move built from the coin's own range,
not a nervous discount.

And the pennies that end in .00 stopped getting shaved down to
whole dollars; the decimals stay where they belong. The Feds,
who love a clean round number, would have kept the haircut and
called it prudence. We call it a ceiling that finally tells
the truth.

---

## 🛡️ WireGuard: The Resolver Stops Hoarding

The resolver had a hoarding problem. Every time the tunnel
woke, OpenRiot reached in and appended another nameserver line
to resolv.conf — on top of what wg-quick and resolvd had
already written — and the file filled with duplicates until it
bulged with its own echo. The machine was doing someone else's
job, and doing it badly. Now it does nothing. wg-quick
registers the tunnel DNS, resolvd blends it in, and OpenRiot
keeps its hands off the file entirely. The duplicates are
gone, and they stay gone.
