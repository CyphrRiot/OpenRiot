# OpenRiot v8.0.7: Thirty-Two Walls

> _"The gallery had fifty pictures and one sentence to say.
> We took the sentence out of the crate, hung it in the middle,
> and let the rest of the frames find their own room. What
> stayed on the wall was not the loudest work. It was the work
> that survived being looked at twice."_
> -- The OpenRiot Crew, v8.0.7

---

OpenRiot 8.0.7 is a deaccession notice with better lighting.

The wallpaper collection stops pretending that more is the same
as better. Fifty images become thirty-two. Every survivor was
rehung, reordered, and re-encoded, so the folder that ships is
smaller than the one that came before it even though nothing
was thrown away for being difficult to look at.

The intake desk also loosened its collar. `openriot
--convert-webp` now accepts JPEG as well as PNG, because the
world did not stop making JPEGs while we were busy renaming
suffixes. `libwebp` joins the desktop package list so the
converter has something to lean on when `cwebp` is the tool
doing the work.

**Total changes: one gallery cull, one rehang, one wider
intake door, and a smaller suitcase.**

---

## The Deaccession

A wallpaper set grows the way a drawer grows. Somebody finds a
good image, it gets a number, and the number is a promise that
it will be there forever. Nothing in that arrangement asks the
obvious question: is anybody still looking at this?

So the wall committee did the unfashionable thing and counted.
Fifty numbered files, every one willing to render, none of them
required to earn its place. The cut is not a judgment on the
art. It is a judgment on the shelf. Thirty-two images fill a set
without turning the picker into a filing cabinet, and thirty-two
is enough for the rotation to feel like a rotation instead of a
slideshow with a bad memory.

`backgrounds/33.webp` through `backgrounds/50.webp` are gone.
The directory is tidier, the checkout is lighter, and nobody
has to scroll past a wall they stopped noticing a year ago.

---

## The Rehang

Deleting was the easy half. The surviving files were not merely
left alone; they were put back in an order that reads.

The sequence now walks from the first frame to the last with
some intent, and several images that used to sit near the end
moved up because they belonged near the front. If you had a
favorite number memorized, this release has opinions about your
memory. The images are still in the box. The labels moved.

Encoding came next. The collection was re-optimized for WebP,
and the difference is not subtle. One frame that used to weigh
734 KB now ships at 349 KB. Another went from 658 KB to 287 KB.
Across the set, roughly 14.8 MB of wallpapers replace a folder
that had grown comfortable at twice the weight. Same pictures,
same colors, fewer bytes spent saying them.

No subscription, no cloud gallery, no "your wallpapers are
available in the app." A file on your disk, in your resolution,
that survives the next reboot and the next company that decides
wallpapers are a service. The wall does not need an account.

---

## The Intake Desk

`openriot --convert-webp` was built for PNG and only PNG. That
was a fine vow until someone handed it a JPEG.

The converter now globs `*.png`, `*.jpg`, and `*.jpeg` in both
`backgrounds/` and `Locked/`, strips whichever suffix it finds,
and writes the `.webp` sibling. `cwebp` gets first refusal. If
it cannot make the trip, ImageMagick's `convert` still catches
the fall, and the original is only removed after a successful
conversion. A file that refuses to convert stays where it is
and says so on stderr.

The command description grew a brace to match: `backgrounds/
*.{png,jpg}` and `Locked/*.{png,jpg}`, because a help string
that lies is just a rumor with formatting.

`install/packages.yaml` now carries `libwebp-1.6.0` in the
desktop set. The converter had been calling `cwebp` and hoping
the base system felt generous. Now the dependency is written
down, installed on purpose, and less likely to be discovered
missing at the worst possible moment.
